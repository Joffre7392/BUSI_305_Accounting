How combining uncorrelated assets reduces portfolio risk

The Key Insight

When you combine uncorrelated assets, the expected return is the average of individual returns, but the risk (standard deviation) decreases by a factor of 1/√n where n is the number of assets. This is because uncorrelated fluctuations tend to cancel each other out.

Risk Reduction with More Assets

Portfolio risk decreases as you add more uncorrelated assets (each with equal weight and equal individual risk).

The Mathematics Behind It

For n uncorrelated assets with equal weights (1/n) and equal variance σ²:

Portfolio Variance = Σ(1/n)² × σ² = n × (1/n²) × σ² = σ²/n

Portfolio Std Dev = σ/√n

Why Does This Work?

  • •When assets are uncorrelated, their random fluctuations are independent
  • •Positive surprises in one asset offset negative surprises in another
  • •The more independent sources of randomness, the more "averaging out" occurs

Important Caveats

  • •Real assets are rarely perfectly uncorrelated
  • •Correlations can increase during market stress
  • •Diversification reduces risk, not expected return

Intuition: The Law of Large Numbers

This is the same principle behind why casinos are profitable even though individual bets are random. With many independent bets, the average outcome converges to the expected value. Similarly, with many uncorrelated investments, the portfolio return becomes more predictable (lower variance) while maintaining the same expected return.
