Lectures 12–13 — Patents, trademarks, goodwill & impairment

Lectures 12-13

What Are Intangible Assets?

Intangible assets are long-lived assets that lack physical existence and are not financial instruments. They represent legal rights or competitive advantages.

Categories

Marketing-Related

Trademarks, brand names, internet domains

Consumer-Related

Customer lists, customer relationships

Artistic-Related

Copyrights, film rights, music

Contract-Related

Licensing agreements, franchise rights

Technology-Related

Patents, software, trade secrets

Goodwill

The “ultimate” intangible — arises only in acquisitions

Accounting Treatment

The key questions: Do we have an accounting asset? and Can we measure its value with reasonable precision?

Acquired Externally → Capitalize

Recognize at acquisition cost on the balance sheet.

Finite Useful Life

Amortize over useful life + annual impairment test

Examples: patents, copyrights, customer lists

Indefinite Useful Life

Do NOT amortize — annual impairment test only

Examples: brand names, trademarks, goodwill

Developed Internally → Expense

General rule: expense when incurred (R&D costs).

Exceptions:

  • US GAAP: Software development costs (after technological feasibility)
  • IFRS: Any development cost (once criteria are met; research is still expensed)

Example: Purchased vs. In-House Patent

Purchased Patent ($100,000, 2-year life)

At acquisition:

Patent (+A)100,000
Cash (−A)100,000

Annual amortization (Years 1 & 2):

Amortization Expense (+E)50,000
Accumulated Amortization50,000

$100,000 ÷ 2 years = $50,000/year

In-House R&D ($100,000 spent)

Entire cost expensed immediately:

R&D Expense (+E)100,000
Cash (−A)100,000

No patent asset on the books! Even though the R&D produced a valuable patent, US GAAP requires immediate expensing because the value cannot be measured with reasonable precision at the time of incurrence.

Impairment of Identifiable Intangibles

Indefinite-life intangibles (like brand names) are not amortized but must be tested annually for impairment.

Impairment Charge = Book Value − Fair Value

FV ≥ BV = No impairment  |  FV < BV = Impairment

Example: SatCo Trademark Impairment

After 5 years, regulatory changes cause SatCo’s $62,000 trademark to become permanently impaired. Fair value = $40,000.

Impairment = $62,000 − $40,000 = $22,000

Loss Due to Impairment (+E, −SE)22,000
Trademark (−A)22,000
=
Back to the Equation

Purchased intangibles sit on the left side of A = L + SE. Amortization gradually moves the cost to the income statement — reducing Assets (via Accumulated Amortization, a contra-asset) and SE (via lower Net Income → lower Retained Earnings). Internally developed intangibles bypass the balance sheet entirely — R&D expense directly reduces SE through the income statement. Impairment write-downs reduce both A↓ and SE↓ in the same period.

Goodwill

Goodwill = Acquisition Price − Fair Value of Net Assets Acquired

What Goodwill Represents

  • Non-identifiable assets: synergies, human capital, growth opportunities
  • Or sometimes … overpayment

Key Rules

  • Only recorded when a business is acquired
  • Is non-transferable — cannot be bought separately
  • Has an indefinite life — not amortized
  • Annual impairment test (cannot be “written up”)

Variant: 75% Acquisition (Non-Controlling Interest)

How is NCI = $40?

Implied total value = $120 ÷ 75% = $160. NCI = 25% × $160 = $40

Where does NCI go?

Non-Controlling Interest is reported within Stockholders’ Equity, separate from the parent’s equity.

Back to the Equation

Acquisitions increase Assets (all acquired assets at FV + goodwill) and Liabilities (all assumed liabilities at FV). Cash decreases, keeping A = L + SE. When stock is issued, Contributed Capital (SE) increases. When only a partial acquisition, Non-Controlling Interest (also in SE) accounts for the minority stake. The equation always balances.

Setup

Example 3: Trademark Impairment

Setup

NovaBrand acquired a trademark 3 years ago for $95,000 (indefinite life — no amortization). Due to a competitor’s product launch, management estimates the trademark’s fair value is now $58,000.

Step 1: Test for Impairment

Book Value = $95,000  |  Fair Value = $58,000

FV ($58,000) < BV ($95,000) → Impaired!

Step 2: Record Impairment

Impairment = $95,000 − $58,000 = $37,000

Loss Due to Impairment37,000
Trademark37,000

New carrying value = $58,000. This loss hits the income statement immediately. The trademark cannot be written back up if the fair value later recovers.
