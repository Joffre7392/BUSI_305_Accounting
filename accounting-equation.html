The Accounting Equation & Financial Statements

Lecture 1 — Foundations of Financial Accounting

What is Accounting?

Accounting is the language of business — a system for recording, summarizing, and reporting economic transactions to help decision-makers allocate resources.

F Financial Accounting

Reports to external users (investors, creditors, regulators). Governed by GAAP/IFRS. Focuses on historical, verifiable data in standardized financial statements.

M Managerial Accounting

Reports to internal users (managers, executives). Not regulated. Focuses on forward-looking budgets, cost analysis, and performance metrics.

The Four Financial Statements

Balance Sheet (BS)

Point in Time

A snapshot of what the company owns (Assets), owes (Liabilities), and the residual claim of owners (Shareholders' Equity) on a specific date.

Income Statement (IS)

Period of Time

Measures profitability over a period: revenues earned minus expenses incurred, resulting in net income (or net loss).

Statement of Cash Flows (SCF)

Period of Time

Tracks actual cash inflows and outflows during the period, categorized into Operating, Investing, and Financing activities.

Statement of Shareholders' Equity (SSE)

Period of Time

Reconciles the beginning and ending equity balances, showing stock issuances, net income, dividends, and other changes.

Accounting Standards & Regulation

GAAP U.S.

Generally Accepted Accounting Principles, set by the FASB (Financial Accounting Standards Board). Required for U.S. public companies.

IFRS International

International Financial Reporting Standards, set by the IASB (International Accounting Standards Board). Used in 140+ countries.

SEC Filings Regulatory

10-K (annual report), 10-Q (quarterly report), 8-K (material events). Filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

This Equation Powers Everything

Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity

Every topic in this course is a specific application of the same equation. Whenever you encounter a new concept, map it back to this framework:

Transactions & Journal Entries

Every entry keeps A = L + SE in balance. Debits = Credits is just another way of saying the same thing.

Inventory & COGS

Splits costs between Assets (left) and Expenses→RE (right). FIFO/LIFO just change how much goes to each side.

Revenue Recognition

Revenue ↑ NI → RE ↑ (right side). An asset increases or a liability decreases on the left/right.

Depreciation

Moves value from PP&E (Asset) to Expense → lower NI → lower RE. Asset shrinks, SE shrinks equally.

Bonds & Leases

Issuing a bond: Cash (A) ↑ and Liability ↑. Interest expense: Cash ↓ and RE ↓. The equation tracks it all.

Financial Analysis

Every ratio (ROA, ROE, D/E, current ratio) is built from equation components. Understanding the equation means understanding what drives the ratios.

Net Income from the Income Statement flows into Retained Earnings, which is a component of Shareholders' Equity on the Balance Sheet. This is the primary link between the two statements.

Statement of Cash Flows

Categorizes all cash inflows and outflows during the period into three activities.

Operating

Cash from core business activities.

  • + Cash collected from customers
  • − Cash paid to suppliers
  • − Cash paid to employees
  • − Cash paid for interest & taxes

Investing

Cash from long-term asset transactions.

  • − Purchase of PP&E
  • + Sale of PP&E
  • − Purchase of investments
  • + Proceeds from investments

Financing

Cash from capital structure changes.

  • + Issuing stock
  • − Repurchasing stock
  • + Borrowing (new debt)
  • − Repaying debt
  • − Paying dividends
Net Change in Cash = Operating + Investing + Financing → reconciles to Cash on the Balance Sheet

Financial Statement Footnotes

The notes to financial statements are an integral part of the financial statements. They provide:

Accounting Policies

Revenue recognition methods, depreciation methods, inventory valuation (FIFO vs LIFO), consolidation policies.

Expanded Detail

Breakdowns of aggregated line items, debt maturity schedules, segment information, pension assumptions.

Off-Balance-Sheet Info

Contingent liabilities, operating lease commitments (pre-ASC 842), guarantees, and other commitments not on the BS.

Step-by-Step Walkthrough

Step 1

Verify the Accounting Equation

$760,000 = $295,000 + $465,000 ✓
Total Assets = Total Liabilities + Total SE

This must be true. If it doesn't balance, there's an error somewhere. Always check this first.

= Back to the Equation

The Balance Sheet is the accounting equation. Every line item on the BS lives inside A = L + SE.

Step 2

Trace Net Income from the Income Statement to the Balance Sheet

Net Income from the Income Statement: $150,000

Check via the Retained Earnings rollforward:

Beginning RE $55,000 + NI $150,000 − Dividends $40,000 = Ending RE $165,000 ✓

$55,000 + $150,000 − $40,000 = $165,000 — matches the Balance Sheet.

Net Income is the bridge between the two statements. It flows from the Income Statement into Retained Earnings on the Balance Sheet.

= Back to the Equation

Revenue and Expenses (IS) determine Net Income, which changes Retained Earnings, which is part of SE in A = L + SE.

Step 3

Identify Statement Types

Balance Sheet

Point in Time

Snapshot as of December 31, 2025.

Shows where the company stands.

Income Statement

Period of Time

Covers January 1 – December 31, 2025.

Shows how the company got there.

The BS shows where the company stands. The IS shows how it got there.

= Back to the Equation

The BS is A = L + SE at a point in time. The IS explains the change in one component of SE (Retained Earnings) over the period.

Step 4

Classify Every Account

  • CashCurrent

  • Equipment (net)Non-current

Every account lives in exactly one bucket: A, L, or SE. Being able to classify accounts is the first step to understanding any transaction.

Step 5

Compute Key Ratios (Preview of Financial Analysis)

Current Ratio

Current Assets / Current Liabilities

= ($180K + $120K + $95K + $15K) / ($65K + $30K)

= $410,000 / $95,000

= 4.3x

Debt-to-Equity Ratio

Total Liabilities / Total SE

= $295,000 / $465,000

= 0.63x

Gross Margin

Gross Profit / Revenue

= $510,000 / $850,000

= 60.0%

Profit Margin

Net Income / Revenue

= $150,000 / $850,000

= 17.6%

= Back to the Equation

Every ratio is built from components of A = L + SE. Current ratio is Current A / Current L. Debt-to-equity is L / SE. Understanding the equation means understanding the building blocks of every ratio.

Putting It All Together

Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity

1. The Balance Sheet is the equation. Total Assets must equal Total Liabilities plus Total SE.

2. Net Income is the bridge. It flows from the IS into Retained Earnings on the BS, connecting the two statements.

3. RE rolls forward: Beginning RE + NI − Dividends = Ending RE.

4. Every account has a home inside A, L, or SE. Classification is not trivia — it determines where things appear on the statements.

5. Every ratio is built from equation components. Current ratio, D/E, margins — they all pull from A, L, SE, or their sub-components.

Master the equation, and the rest of accounting is applications.
